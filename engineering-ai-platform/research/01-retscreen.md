# RETScreen Expert

RETScreen Expert is Natural Resources Canada’s Windows desktop clean-energy management platform. Its stated scope spans energy-efficiency, renewable-energy and cogeneration feasibility analysis, ongoing performance analysis, and multi-facility portfolio management. The official product page says it is used to analyze, plan, implement, monitor and report on energy projects, with global climate data and other databases supporting decisions. [1]

## Capabilities and workflow

The core workflow is project- or facility-centered. Users can start from facility archetypes in the Virtual Energy Analyzer, including power plants, commercial and institutional buildings, residential facilities, cogeneration, and individual efficiency measures such as lighting, motors and pumps. [2] The official subscription page lists a Benchmark/Feasibility workflow with energy, emissions, power/storage/off-grid, transportation, user-defined and other analysis modules, plus facility meter information, scaling/calibration, cost and electricity-rate databases, and GHG-emissions factors. [3]

RETScreen’s lifecycle analysis is organized around benchmark, feasibility, performance and portfolio use cases. The feasibility process covers energy, cost, emissions, financial, and sensitivity/risk analysis; the platform also supports comparing facilities with reference or base years, calculating cumulative savings, ranking facilities, and graphing energy, emissions, fuel and intensity measures across a portfolio. [2] [4]

The product is designed for rapid screening rather than a complete engineering design environment. Its value proposition is to reduce the cost and time of identifying and evaluating clean-energy opportunities before implementation, then measure and verify actual performance after implementation. [1] [2]

## Interoperability and operating model

RETScreen Expert is a Windows desktop application requiring Windows 8.1, 10 or 11 and .NET Framework 4.8 or higher. [1] Professional mode explicitly includes save/save-as, portfolio and template saving, autosave, clipboard copying, import from file, export/export to file, email sending, printing, custom worksheets, and database access. [3] These are useful file- and worksheet-level interoperability mechanisms, but the cited official materials do not document a public API, cloud-native collaboration model, standards-based engineering exchange, or direct integrations with enterprise systems. The product should therefore be treated as desktop/file-centric unless additional documentation establishes otherwise. [1] [3]

## Pricing approach

Viewer mode is free. Professional mode is a renewable 12-month subscription currently priced at CAD 869 per subscription, plus applicable Canadian taxes. The subscription activates after payment through a software key; the official order page says the key is normally emailed within two business days and is not auto-renewed or stored as a recurring card charge. [1] [3]

One paid subscription permits the subscribing organization to install the key on up to ten computers for employees. Educational institutions can use one annual subscription on unlimited organization-owned and currently registered student-owned computers for non-commercial teaching and research. [3] The model is therefore freemium for viewing and paid annual licensing for productive authoring/export and advanced features, with unusually favorable education terms. [3]

## Weaknesses and limitations

The main structural limitations are desktop dependence, a gated save/print/export workflow in Viewer mode, and a file-oriented interoperability model. The official sources also show that many advanced analyses and databases are tied to Professional mode. [1] [3] This can create friction for distributed teams, browser/mobile users, automated pipelines, and organizations that need shared system-of-record data rather than local workbooks.

Independent G2 feedback is positive overall (4.7/5 from 13 reviews), but users report that the many tabs and information density can be overwhelming initially, the interface looks somewhat dated, help can be difficult to use, and the tool may not provide advanced analysis for a highly technical presentation. Other reviewers mention occasional lags/shutdowns and that the license price is expensive for part-time consultants. These are user reports, not independently verified product defects, but they are relevant signals about onboarding, UX, depth and affordability. [5]

## Product opportunity for a unified AI-native engineering platform

RETScreen demonstrates demand for a guided, lifecycle-oriented energy decision workflow that combines archetypes, climate/resource data, engineering calculations, financial analysis, emissions, risk, benchmarking and post-implementation measurement. A unified AI-native platform could preserve that decision flow while improving discoverability through conversational project setup, explainable assumptions, automatic data validation, scenario generation, and traceable links from inputs to calculations and reports.

The clearest whitespace is around collaboration and interoperability: a browser-first, multi-user workspace with versioned models, reusable templates, permissioned portfolio data, API/connectors, and explicit import/export schemas could remove the desktop/file bottleneck. AI should assist rather than obscure engineering judgment: every generated assumption, benchmark, forecast or recommendation should expose provenance, uncertainty, equations and editable inputs. RETScreen’s pricing and G2 feedback also suggest opportunities for a lower-friction team/consultant tier, transparent usage limits, and a simplified learning mode alongside professional depth. These are product inferences from the documented workflow and reported user experience, not claims about RETScreen’s roadmap.
